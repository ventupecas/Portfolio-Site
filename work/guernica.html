Case study

In Memory of Guernica

An 18-piece jigsaw of Picasso’s Guernica for a tech-expo booth. Every piece you place reveals a chapter of the Spanish Civil War and its poets, in Spanish and English.

  • JavaScript
  • Canvas
  • Pointer Events
  • Web Audio
Play the puzzle ← All projects

Why I made it

The bombing of Guernica shows what violence costs, and why peaceful, non-violent action is worth remembering. I wanted people to rebuild that memory with their own hands, one piece at a time.

Built for a tech expo. Picasso painted Guernica for one too: the 1937 Paris International Exposition, devoted to arts and technology.

How it works

  1. Visitors pick Spanish or English and press Start.
  2. Each piece placed correctly reveals the next chapter, in date order: the history of the war, and poems by Lorca, Alberti, Cernuda, Guillén and Aleixandre.
  3. Finishing plays a sound and shows Lorca’s poem “Despedida”.
  4. After two minutes without anyone touching it, the puzzle starts over with a new shuffle for the next visitor.

Decisions I made

  • Built for a booth: nothing to install and no internet needed. It runs by opening one file, even offline.
  • Several people can drag pieces at the same time, with a mouse or on a touch screen.
  • The Spanish is checked against sources from Spain, like the RAE, and every poem excerpt is checked word for word against a published edition.
  • Poets whose work is still under copyright are quoted briefly, with the author, poem and book named.

The hard part: jigsaw-shaped pieces

Each piece is its own canvas, cut along a jigsaw outline. Because pieces overlap, the board decides which one you grabbed by testing your finger or cursor against each outline. Positions are stored in grid units, so resizing the screen doesn’t scatter the pieces.

In numbers
